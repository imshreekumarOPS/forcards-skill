title: "A field guide on how to ==write clear emails=="

## Lead with the ask
- Put the one thing you need from the reader in the first two lines.
- "Can you approve the Q3 budget by Friday?" beats three paragraphs of background that end in a question.
- Readers skim on a phone, so the opening decides whether the rest gets read at all.
- If the ask is buried, it will be missed.

## Write subject lines that work alone
- A subject line should tell the reader what the email is and what to do about it.
- "Quick question" says nothing, while "Approve Q3 budget by Friday" says everything.
- Add a deadline or a verb, because both help the reader sort a crowded inbox.
- Someone should be able to act on the subject without opening the email.

## Keep to one topic
- One email should carry one main request or one decision.
- Mixing a budget question with a scheduling question means one of them gets ignored.
- Send two short emails instead of one long one, so each can be answered and filed separately.
- Fewer topics, faster replies.

## Cut the warm-up
- Skip openers such as "I hope this finds you well" and "I wanted to reach out".
- Say who you are only when the reader would not know, and then do it in one line.
- Every sentence before the point is a sentence the reader has to get through.
- Start where the useful part starts.

## Make the next step obvious
- End by stating exactly what happens next and who does it.
- "I will send the draft on Tuesday; please reply with edits by Thursday" names an owner and a date.
- Vague closers like "let me know your thoughts" leave the reader to invent the task.
- If nobody owns the next step, nothing happens.

## Use short paragraphs and plain words
- Keep paragraphs to two or three sentences and leave a blank line between them.
- Swap "utilize" for "use" and "commence" for "start" wherever the meaning stays the same.
- Use a short list when you have three or more parallel items.
- White space is part of the message.

## Add only what the reader needs
- Include the context the reader needs to decide, and link to the rest.
- Attach the document instead of pasting twelve pages into the body.
- Name attachments clearly, for example "q3-budget-v2.pdf" rather than "final_FINAL.pdf".
- If a detail would not change the answer, leave it out.

## Reread before you send
- Read the email once, out loud or in your head, as if you were the recipient.
- Check the ask, the deadline, the names and every attachment you mentioned.
- A thirty-second pass catches most of the mistakes that cause a second email.
- Send it only when you could answer it yourself in ten seconds.
